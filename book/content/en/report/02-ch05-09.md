These sources support three classes of **mutually complementary** methods: TI/Xu/ETRI explain how to preserve the cycle state; CN/Zong/Havens/Sony/Marvell explain how to maintain or recover consistency; Kuo/MediaTek explain physical phase selection and local fine measurement. The gaps in the table are not formatting omissions; they are interface arguments that still have to be completed.

This report does not give a single, unconditional conclusion based on “which publication is the closest match”. If the concern is the /8 split, TI is the most direct; if the concern is shared state and correction, Havens/Sony are more direct; if the concern is physical phase selection for a short TDC, Kuo/MediaTek are more direct; if the concern is the sampling waveforms of a split counter, Zong Fig.9 is the most suitable current entry point for checking. This is a division of labour by question, not a claim that any one publication is a substitute for the complete target.

# 6. Core Difficulty 1: Epoch Errors Must Be Classified by Level

## 6.1 A /8 Group-Boundary Example: Two Legal Values and Two Torn Values

Let the old state be $C=10,q=7$, and let the next raw edge cause the low bits to wrap to zero and the high bits to carry. The corresponding states are:

| State | High bits C | Low bits q | 8C+q | Meaning |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Before the group-boundary crossing | 10 | 7 | 87 | Legal old state |
| After the group-boundary crossing | 11 | 0 | 88 | Legal new state |
| Old high bits + new low bits | 10 | 0 | 80 | Off by −8 relative to the new state 88 |
| New high bits + old low bits | 11 | 7 | 95 | Off by +8 relative to the old state 87 |

This example does not simulate any particular patent; it is a fault-injection derivation under Eq. (1). It proves that when the fault is **high-low word tearing**, a corrector that allows only ±1 raw cycle cannot cover the two errors above.

When compared against the legal anchor on the other side, the difference may also appear as ±7. Therefore it must first be specified which old/new epoch should be associated; the correction class cannot be inferred backwards from the absolute value of the error alone.

## 6.2 Defining Raw-Cycle Correction and Block Correction Separately

It is recommended that the phase-integration interface be written as:

$$
N_{\mathrm{corr}}=M(C_{\mathrm{cap}}+\kappa_C)+q_{\mathrm{corr}}+\kappa_N. \tag{10}
$$

Here the unit of $\kappa_C$ is one slow count group, and the unit of $\kappa_N$ is one raw CKV cycle; $q_{\mathrm{corr}}$ may further be a residue reconstructed from legal codes/states. Do not use a single unitless `kappa` to mix three responsibilities.

$\kappa_C\in\{-1,0,+1\}$ is reasonable only when the sampling delay and the synchronization delay have been proven to span at most one group. If the capture can be delayed by multiple groups, the correction set must be extended, or the observation must be declared invalid outright. The correction range is a specification derived from timing upper bounds, not an encoding width chosen arbitrarily in advance.

## 6.3 Why “Looking at q or the Raw Parity” May Completely Miss a High-Bit Group Error

For $M=4$ or 8:

$$
(N+M)\bmod M=N\bmod M,
$$

and moreover:

$$
(N+M)\bmod2=N\bmod2. \tag{11}
$$

Therefore an error of one whole group changes neither q nor the parity of the raw cycle. Transplanting one of Havens/Sony's low-bit XORs directly into the /8 model will not necessarily detect a high-bit group error.

To detect a block mismatch, additional state that toggles across groups must be retained, for example an independent observation of the least significant bit of the high bits, a wrap toggle/sequence, or a capture protocol that guarantees the high bits are taken from a determined candidate epoch. Even if high-bit parity is used, it can discriminate only when **the error has already been confined to an adjacent group**; a difference of two groups aliases again. This is a question of the coverage of the redundant code.

## 6.4 The Boundary Admits Two Coordinates, but the Two Chains Must Not Each Choose a Different One

Suppose the reference lies right near the adjacent edges J and J+1. The following two phase representations are identical:

$$
J-\mu=(J+1)-(\mu+1). \tag{12}
$$

Therefore a valid coarse/fine handover does not necessarily need to select a unique, pre-designated edge every time. What it needs is that the selected integer anchor and the fine interval **transform together**. Only when the integer takes J+1 while the fine value is still relative to J is a one-cycle error produced.

Eq. (12) is an exact phase-domain identity. When a long time interval is normalized by a single average period, the approximation conditions of Section 3.3 must also be satisfied. Algebraic consistency must not be mistaken for exactness under an arbitrary time-domain model.

## 6.5 Reasonable Boundaries for Metastability Claims

A Gray/Johnson state with single-bit changes can constrain the adjacency of the digital codes, but it does not free the sampling flip-flop from the risk of analog metastability. Delay matching and dual-candidate selection likewise depend on the devices, routing, duty cycle and safe window.

This report adopts two-level acceptance: within the defined digital delay/boundary model, prove that no silent wrong code exists; within transistor-level and PVT models, separately evaluate metastability propagation, decision delay and the residual failure rate. For now, the functional description in any patent is not taken as a verification result for this project's “arbitrary phase, absolutely zero failures”.

# 7. Core Difficulty 2: Three Implementation Routes for the Snapshot and Their Timing Constraints

## 7.1 Route A: Multi-Candidate Capture and Safe-Path Selection

The idea is to generate different reference-retiming candidates from the rising/falling edges or from multiple phases, capture the phase information with each candidate, and then select the safe candidate based on boundary-side information. TI and Kuo provide related direct implementations; Havens/Marvell show the importance of additional state judgments. [R01][R06][R08][R10]

In engineering terms, three things must be distinguished: whether the data candidates are stable, whether the selection control is stable, and whether the entire tuple after selection comes from the same candidate. It is not acceptable to have only each candidate register meet setup/hold while using a select signal that may arrive late or be metastable to combinationally switch a wide bus.

It is recommended to take the “candidate tuple” as the unit of selection: `{coarse, low_state, boundary_side, fine_anchor_id, candidate_id}`. A valid event is output only after selection, rather than coarse selecting A while fine defaults to B.

## 7.2 Route B: Matching the Sampling Instants Along the Count-Propagation Direction

Let the data of group i correspond to the target logical edge $t_E$, with its propagation delay lying in $[d_i^{min},d_i^{max}]$, and let the sampling time be $t_{S,i}$. The necessary constraints are:

$$
 t_{S,i}^{min}\geq t_E+d_i^{max}+t_{su,i}, \tag{13}
$$

$$
 t_{S,i}^{max}+t_{h,i}\leq t_{next,i}+d_{next,i}^{min}. \tag{14}
$$

Here $t_{next,i}$ is the next logical event that may overwrite this group, and it is not necessarily equal to $t_E+T_0$ for every group. More importantly, all groups must target the same logical epoch; making every group “wait until it is stable before sampling” does not guarantee that the same beat is captured.

The ideal matching in Zong Fig.9 provides a relative readout position of about half a CKV cycle. When transplanting it to this project, the actual margins should be computed:

$$
 m_i=t_{S,i}^{min}-t_E-d_i^{max}-t_{su,i},\quad
 h_i=t_{next,i}+d_{next,i}^{min}-t_{S,i}^{max}-t_{h,i}. \tag{15}
$$

The acceptance condition is $m_i>0,h_i>0$ for every group, with all groups belonging to the same epoch. [R05][R11]

## 7.3 Route C: Bounded Mismatch and Redundant Correction

Havens/Sony show that an integer capture ambiguity of a known type can be tolerated and then identified and corrected from the fractional state. But the set of “all possible error states” must be established first, rather than first choosing an XOR and then hoping it covers all errors. [R07][R08]

For /8, the minimum requirement is to list separately: legal adjacent changes of the low bits, a high-bit error of one group, high/low-bit tearing, the fine anchor being delayed by one raw cycle, phase-selector wrap-around, and synchronizer delay exceeding the assumed range. Different errors may require different redundant information and different invalidity handling.

## 7.4 For All Three Routes, a Module Name Cannot Replace a Complete Proof

| Route | Problem it can directly solve | Still requires independent proof |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-candidate | Avoids dangerous sampling positions near specific boundaries | Select stability, atomic tuple selection, fixed/varying delays |
| Matched delay | Count bits propagating non-simultaneously | PVT min/max, the same logical epoch, reset and sampling-pulse width |
| Redundant correction | Bounded, identifiable epoch inconsistencies | Fault-set coverage, correction unit, INVALID on ambiguity |

These methods can be combined, but after combination the joint state space must be re-checked. One cannot simply add one publication's safe retimer to another publication's correction and conclude that all boundaries are thereby closed.

# 8. Core Difficulty 3: Causality of P/S and Physical Edge Execution

## 8.1 The System Semantics of P/S Should Be “Execute a Designated Edge”

The target edge index is decomposed as:

$$
J_{\mathrm{plan}}=MH_J+s_J,\qquad s_J\in\{0,\ldots,M-1\}. \tag{16}
$$

The slow domain handles high-bit matching, future planning and parallel control; a small amount of high-speed state is responsible for slot / phase-local enable, pulse formation and root-clock retiming. The continuous CNT still independently records the $CK_0$ cycles that have actually elapsed.

The recommended functional data flow is as follows. It is an architectural synthesis made in this report, not a redrawing of an original figure from any publication.

```text
actual CK0 → continuous high/low count state → REF-related safe snapshot → coarse anchor
                     │
plan J_plan → high-bit hit + slot control → physical edge selection/gating → actual edge
                                                                                 │
                                                  execution-side observation J_observed
                                                                                 │
REF → local fine measurement ← selected true edge ───────────────────────────────┘
                     │
        tuple consistency + VALID/SAT + coordinate reconstruction → phase error
```

## 8.2 The Plan Must Not Be Treated as Evidence of Execution

`J_plan` and `J_observed` must be distinguished. The latter is formed from the actual execution path, trustworthy high-speed state or an edge receipt; one must not delay the plan word by a few beats and rename it `J_exec`.

The reason is that a wrong edge, a missing edge, a duplicate edge or late gate control may leave the digital plan completely unchanged. If the measurement chain uses only the command value, its reconstructed phase may look correct in the model while the true physical edge has already shifted by one beat.

The execution receipt may come from a stable count shadow corresponding to the target slot, a controlled launch acknowledgement, or a post-matching state; the specific circuit is still to be determined. The fixed pipeline offset between the receipt itself and the actual output must also be traceable.

## 8.3 An After-the-Fact Low-Speed Snapshot Cannot Generate a Nearest Edge That Has Already Passed

This is a causality question that any candidate scheme must answer explicitly: after REF arrives, by the time it has gone through snapshot, CDC, decode and low-speed computation and then controls the selector, a given CKV edge near REF may long since have passed. Even if q is computed perfectly correctly, time cannot be turned back to send that edge into the TDC again.

Only three classes of implementation paths can close this gap: predicting in advance and committing the selection before the target; having a high-speed front end capture candidate/local timing information in parallel and then decoding it at low speed; or choosing a future edge that can still be executed, and accepting and calibrating its time interval and range requirement.

Kuo's time-predictive phase selection belongs to the route with a priori planning; it must not be understood as “after the reference arrives, finish the computation at an arbitrarily slow pace and then select, at no cost, the nearest phase that has just passed”. [R06]

## 8.4 Edge-Selection Deadline and Late Policy

Suppose the selection control must be established $t_{\mathrm{setup,exec}}$ before the target edge, and the command arrives at the executor at time $t_{\mathrm{arrive}}$; then it is required that:

$$
t_{\mathrm{arrive}}+t_{\mathrm{setup,exec}}+t_{\mathrm{guard}}\leq t_{\mathrm{target}}. \tag{17}
$$

If this is not satisfied, one must not silently reissue in the next slot while still returning the original edge ID. One must explicitly adopt `LATE/MISS`, re-plan a different J, or switch the measurement strategy. Any reissue should change the execution receipt and the phase coordinate.

For adjacent events, it must also be guaranteed that two consecutive legal pulses are not merged, lost or duplicated. The level behaviour of a generic PISO outputting consecutive “1”s is not necessarily the same as the two independent sampling edges that are required.

## 8.5 MASH Belongs to the Extension Layer, Not a Condition Smuggled into This Round's Literature-Isomorphism Judgment

The previous-round scheme included a MASH future edge. The basic problem in this round is first to resolve the correspondence among the continuous CNT, the /M residue, the snapshot and the selected-edge fine measurement. MASH only supplies a target sequence on top of this interface; its order, state range, dither and feedback prediction error still need to be quantified separately.

One cannot deny a source's direct contribution to the basic measurement problem merely because it has no MASH; nor can one consider the raw-cycle CNT and fine interface to be naturally solved merely because a MASH divider has been found.

# 9. Core Difficulty 4: True Fine Range, Saturation and Calibration

## 9.1 The Large Range of the CNT and the Small Physical Range of the TDC Are Two Different Responsibilities

The CNT can represent a multi-cycle difference between the reference and the current state, and can maintain the coarse observation through lock-acquisition and tracking transients; but it does not change the physical time interval that already exists between the two TDC inputs. The range of a short TDC must be constrained by mechanisms such as actual edge selection, the capture structure, prediction or a DTC. [R06][R09][R11]

Suppose a saturation function $Q(x)$ outputs R for all $x\geq R$; then $Q(1.5R)=Q(3R)=R$. Knowing only this output, the original input cannot be recovered. Subtracting some whole number of cycles afterwards cannot recover fine information that has already been compressed into the same code either.

Therefore “the digital side can take a modulo” does not prove that “the analog TDC can be shrunk to one cycle”. The former is a matter of numerical representation; the latter is a matter of physical observability.

## 9.2 Path Delay, DTC and the Signs of Normalization

Define the actual time difference at the TDC pins:

$$
d_{\mathrm{pin}}=(t_E+\tau_E)-(t_R+\tau_R).
$$

The original event interval to be recovered is:

$$
\widehat{\Delta t}=\widehat d_{\mathrm{pin}}-(\widehat\tau_E-\widehat\tau_R). \tag{18}
$$

If the DTC is inserted in the reference path, its delay is counted in $\tau_R$; if it is inserted in the feedback path, it is counted in $\tau_E$. The path definition of Eq. (18) must be used uniformly; one cannot treat the DTC as already compensated in one place and then add it again in another.

The time-LSB normalization is:

$$
\widehat\mu=(\mathrm{code}-\mathrm{code}_0)\frac{\mathrm{LSB}_t}{\widehat T_0}+o_{\mathrm{phase}}. \tag{19}
$$

Here $o_{\mathrm{phase}}$ is the remaining calibrated phase offset. `LSB_t`, `code_0`, the phase-selection/DCC deviations, the differential path delays and the period estimate must carry a calibration version. Treating the raw code directly as a fraction of the raw cycle may hide gain errors or clock-node mistakes inside the phase loop.

## 9.3 Conditions for Adopting the Fine Value

Recommended:

$$
F_{\mathrm{usable}}=V_{\mathrm{code}}\land V_{\mathrm{range}}\land V_{\mathrm{anchor}}
\land V_{\mathrm{epoch}}\land V_{\mathrm{cal}}. \tag{20}
$$

Here each V denotes the corresponding validity criterion, and $F_{\mathrm{usable}}$ corresponds to the interface signal `fine_usable`. `range_ok` must come from saturate/overflow/window checks that the circuit can actually implement, not from an ideal true phase that is available in a simulator but invisible to the circuit. `anchor_ok`, in turn, requires that the physical edge actually selected be consistent with its index.

One-sided saturation may still retain “error direction” information. Such information can take part in a separately defined clamped control strategy, such as Zong's transient handling, but it must not be labelled as an exact fine residual. [R11]

## 9.4 Coarse Takeover Does Not Mean Deleting Fine, Still Less Adding an FLL Out of Nowhere

When fine is invalid but coarse is still reliable, a defined coarse phase estimate together with its quantization-error upper bound may be used; or the sign/interval information may be retained and fed into a controlled strategy. If coarse cannot be determined either, this observation must be invalid, rather than being forcibly stitched together.

Take the reference accumulated phase $R[k]$ and the coarse observation $N_R[k]$ as an example:

$$
e_c[k]=R[k]-N_R[k]. \tag{21}
$$

This is a coarsely quantized **phase error**. Only its difference corresponds to:

$$
e_c[k]-e_c[k-1]=FCW[k]-(N_R[k]-N_R[k-1]). \tag{22}
$$

So the CNT does not inherently imply an independent FLL. Only when a frequency-error controller, a state machine or a gain path is separately designed should it be called FLL-assisted control.

When fine recovers, the consistency of the coordinate offset and the continuity of the control state must also be checked; switching phase_source must not introduce an artificial whole-cycle jump. Freezing the integrator, clamping the integrator, or staged re-admission are all strategies that require closed-loop verification; this document does not presuppose that an optimal choice has already been made.

## 9.5 A Numerical Example Illustrating the Range

The following is an idealized calculation example, not a chip specification: with a raw $f_0=8$ GHz, $T_0=125$ ps. The /4 period is 500 ps; the uniform eight-phase spacing is 62.5 ps. If the nearest phase can be selected, the ideal signed residual can be kept within half a phase spacing; if the succeeding phase must be selected, the maximum wait approaches one phase spacing.

Beyond these two ideal ranges, one must further add prediction error, phase non-uniformity, path error, transient frequency offset and the decision guard. Even if “eight phases” exist, one cannot write the TDC range directly as ±31.25 ps without explaining the causality of edge selection. The range must be derived from the actual measurement procedure.
